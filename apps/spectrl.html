Spectrl

Spectrum, key detection, note frequencies, tonal balance and a system-audio sampler in one window.

A spectrum analyser on its own is one tool. Spectrl is six, all reading your Mac's audio: a live spectrum, a note frequency table, key detection, loudness meters, an oscilloscope, a tonal balance target curve and a system audio sampler. One light window instead of six plugins.

Six tools, one signal

The note ruler at the top of the window answers "what note is that?". Use it to see which frequency a note sits at, or which note a frequency is.

Tonal balance draws a corridor over the spectrum showing where mastered tracks in a genre usually sit, with a dashed line down the middle. The corridor shows shape, not loudness. It lines up with your own level, so a quiet mix and a loud one with the same tilt sit in the same place.

Learn does the same with your own music. Play a reference track or your own song, press Learn, and its curve becomes the target you are compared against. Save it to the preset list to use again.

Key detection. Press the button and Spectrl listens for 10 seconds, then guesses the key and scale of your audio and shows how confident it is.

The REC button samples your system audio in one click. Set the sample folder to your Live User Library, or any folder your DAW can see, and the file is right there in your DAW. After recording, Spectrl also offers the WAV file for you to drag straight into your DAW.

Features

  • Reads in notes, not only hertz. A note ruler under the spectrum, the nearest note to your cursor in the footer, and exact frequencies on hover.
  • Key detection. One press and it listens to ten seconds of audio, then shows the likely key and scale and marks it on the note ruler until you close it. Handy for loops and samples with no key on them.
  • Tonal balance targets. Compare what you hear with a target curve, or with your own song using Learn.
  • Loudness meters and an oscilloscope, frozen together with the spectrum by one key. Freeze lets you hover for exact values.
  • Hold to smooth. Press and hold on the spectrum and the averaging gets 6× longer, so a short peak stays long enough to read. Let go and it is live again.
  • Sample anything your Mac plays, in one click. No routing, no aggregate device, no extra capture app. Press record and Spectrl saves whatever is coming out of your speakers to WAV.
  • As light as you need. FFT resolution up to 4K bins and a refresh rate of 30, 15, 8 or 4 fps, for the spectrum and the meters together. Peak detection always uses the full audio stream, so a low rate only costs smoothness. You can leave it open all session next to a heavy set.

Why standalone

Spectrl runs as its own app, not a plugin, like a rack analyser that sits beside your gear instead of inside it. It reads your Mac's audio directly, so it works with anything that makes sound, not only your DAW. If your DAW crashes, Spectrl keeps running and keeps what it was recording. If Spectrl has a problem, your session is not affected.

Requirements

macOS 14.4 (Sonoma) or later, on Apple Silicon.

Intel Macs: beta. The app is built for Intel too and should work, but it has not been tested on an Intel Mac yet.

The first time it runs, macOS asks for permission to record this computer's audio. Allow it. Without it the screen stays dark and tells you why.

Signed and notarised for macOS, so it opens with a normal double-click, with no security warning.

Only your audio, nothing else

Spectrl listens through a Core Audio process tap, which asks for this computer's audio and nothing else. It never needs Screen Recording permission.

It does not use your microphone, and listening does not mute or change what you hear.
