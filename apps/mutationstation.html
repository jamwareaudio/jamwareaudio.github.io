MutationStation

Lock the notes that work, mutate the rest, and find acid lines you would not have written yourself.

MutationStation and Chordinator share one idea: controlled randomness. When writing note by note runs dry, a tool that pulls you out of your usual patterns helps. Happy accidents are one of the best parts of making electronic music, and this sequencer is built for them. It is an acid-style mutating step sequencer. It can write new sequences from nothing, or mutate ideas you already have, like a bassline or a melody.

At its core is the Elektron-style lock: lock the notes you like and only the rest will mutate. Sliders, parameters and ready-made algorithms set how random the mutation is and what style it has. You can also set a probability for each pitch, for generative sequences that keep evolving.

Start from a line you drew in, dropped in as a MIDI clip, or pulled from Live, and MutationStation rolls new versions of it. You set how far the pitch moves, how the rhythm shifts, and where accents and slides land. Audition takes until one is right, then drag it onto a track.

Controlled randomness

A randomiser that changes everything is a toy. It becomes a writing tool when you can say which parts are already right.

MutationStation uses the lock system Elektron sequencers made famous. Click a note to lock it and every generator leaves it alone. Pitch, rhythm, octave, length, accent and slide all move around it. Lock the root and the turnaround, re-roll the rest as often as you like, and the phrase keeps its shape while the detail changes.

Auto-Lock picks those notes for you. Structural weighs seven things together: recurrence, metric weight, accent, bar openers, note length, low register and isolation. To let one thing decide instead, rank by downbeats, offbeats, accents, long notes, low notes, recurrence, or the phrase corners that hold a bar together.

Or mutate one part and leave the rest alone. Drag in the piano roll to mark a range. From then on every mutation device only changes what is inside it, and notes outside it stay as they are, whatever slider you move. Press Esc to unmark.

Every roll uses seeded dice, so a take you liked comes back exactly, and you can re-roll one panel at a time while the rest stays put.

Where the notes come from

34 pitch strategies, from simple ones (Random, Proximity, Root/Fifth) to acid and techno voices like Acid Trip, Hypno Root, Seventh Anchor, Lead Line and Offbeat Anchor. Many are measured from real bass and synth loops, so they move the way basslines really move, not like a random note generator. Amount sets how much of the line they may rewrite. Scale, root and range set where the new notes may land.

A Phrasing strip sits on the Scale panel, with Phrase Length, End Bias, and a "Which phrases" menu (every phrase, every 2nd, or every 4th) that sets how often the bias applies. Each device also has its own Length and Bias. They follow the strip until you set one yourself, and the LCD's Off entry makes them follow it again. Lengths are even numbers up to 32 bars, and an EXTEND switch grows a device's sequence to fit a longer length.

Slides and accents the way a 303 does them. Octave, Accent and Slide each have their own amount, step pattern and lock. Keep the accents you have and re-roll only the glides, or lock the octave jumps and let everything else move. Connect Notes fills the gaps with passing tones, in scale or chromatic.

Take the rhythm from a clip · audio clips too

↓ Rhythm Import takes the timing of the clip selected in Live and puts it in the roll as a single line on the root note. It is the quickest way to mutate a pattern you already have. Click a clip in Live, press the button, and its rhythm is now your sequence's rhythm.

It works on audio clips as well as MIDI. Live cannot pass audio to an app, so MutationStation reads the sample from disk and finds the hits itself, by looking for sudden jumps in the spectrum from one moment to the next. How hard each hit is becomes its velocity.

≈ Similar to Live leaves your sequence alone and sorts the Pattern list by how close each pattern is to the MIDI clip Live is showing. Click one to load it.

Features

  • Mutate a line across pitch, rhythm, octave, note length, accent and slide. Each has its own amount and works on its own.
  • A note pool you can weight. Twelve keys set which notes mutation may use, and a % panel behind them sets how often each one comes up.
  • 40+ rhythm patterns. Four-on-the-floor, rolling 303, tresillo, dub, house basslines, hypnotic rolls and two-bar call-and-response cells, with a density knob to thin or thicken any of them.
  • Extend Loop. Double or quadruple the loop and let each extra bar drift from the first, with its own amount for pitch, octave, duration, accent and slide.
  • End Bias, on the Phrasing strip with Phrase Length. Every one, two or four bars, the last notes of the group are more likely to mutate. In house and techno, variation usually comes at the end of a phrase, so this helps lines sound written. Each device can override the strip's Length and Bias, and grow to fit a longer length with EXTEND.
  • Keep Original. Last in the chain: after every other panel has had its turn, each unlocked note has a chance to go back to how it started. Some of the take survives heavy mutation without locking anything.
  • A real piano roll. Draw, drag, resize and set velocity. A Snapshots strip keeps every take you generate so you can go back to one you liked, and + Snapshot saves one with its settings. A / B gives two takes of the same settings, and your line is only replaced when you press Commit to Main.
  • Presets and Find Similar Rhythm. Save sequences and knob settings, and search your saved sequences for ones with a similar groove to the one on screen.
  • 50 factory 303-style sequences come with the app, kept apart from your own library.
  • Modulation lanes for Live. Draw up to eight per-step lanes, each moving one parameter on the selected device as smooth 14-bit automation. Play them live, or press Send to Live to make a new Session clip with the notes and the modulation as clip envelopes. One-click presets draw a curve across three parameters at once, and you can save your own. Modulation record works the other way: arm a lane, move a knob in Live, and the lane draws your move.
  • Play and record straight in from a MIDI keyboard or a DAW track. Arm Record and notes land on the grid as you play. Overdub sets whether a new note replaces or adds to the last sequence, and Recapture saves the loop you just played even if nothing was armed. Chord In and Root In read the same port, so a played chord or note transposes the sequence live.
  • Follow your DAW's clock, or run on your own. Pick the port your DAW sends MIDI clock on and the app follows its tempo and position. Pick Internal and it plays by itself.
  • An Info box like Live's, and the manual in the app. The Info box describes whatever the mouse is over: LCD menus, pitch algorithms and every control.
  • Randomise can set a device's Length and Bias too, each on a 50% chance, and always to an even length. Each device has its own ⚄ to pick new settings for that device only, and a re-roll key to reshuffle its dice. Mutated notes get a soft outline in the roll instead of a new colour, so the fill still shows velocity.
  • And more. See the manual for the full list.

Why standalone

MutationStation runs as its own app, not a plugin, and reaches your DAW like a hardware groovebox: as a MIDI device you record from or drag takes out of. If your DAW crashes, MutationStation and every take in its Snapshots strip are safe. If MutationStation crashes, your DAW keeps running.

Requirements

macOS 12 or later on Apple Silicon (M1 or later), and any DAW that takes MIDI.

Intel Macs: beta. The app is built for Intel too and should work, but it has not been tested on an Intel Mac yet.

Signed and notarised for macOS, so it opens with a normal double-click, with no security warning.

Getting it into your DAW

The app shows up on your Mac as a MIDI device called MutationStation. Set a track to listen to it, turn on input monitoring, and play. Or just drag a take onto a track as a .mid clip, which needs no setup at all.

Extra integration with Live

A control script comes with the app, shared by MutationStation, Chordinator and TouchXY. Install it and you get ↓ From Live, which pulls the clip you are looking at into the sequencer, ↓ Rhythm Import, which takes just the rhythm of the clip Live is showing, and spacebar transport, so the app's transport key starts and stops Live.

The other way, Send to Live writes a Session clip with the notes and any modulation lanes you drew as clip automation. Dragging out a MIDI file still works in any DAW, for notes only.

Control it from MidiMirror

MidiMirror can switch a controller into JamWare mode and hand it to MutationStation. Every knob, fader and key gets this app's own parameters, named and with live values, so the MidiMirror panel becomes MutationStation's front panel, with nothing to map by hand.
