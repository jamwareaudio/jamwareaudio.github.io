MidiMirror

A mirror for your MIDI controller: see what every knob is set to, and choose what each one does.

Ever forgotten what each knob on your MIDI controller does? Or wondered what knob 8 is mapped to in device mode? Even on a controller with a display (like the Launch Control XL MK3 or Push), you often only find out what a knob does once you move it.

MidiMirror was made to fix that. It has layout templates that copy the look of your MIDI controller, with every knob, fader and button in the same place. Put MidiMirror on a screen above your controller and you always know what each knob does, anywhere in your Live set.

Two ways to run it: Passive or Active

Every controller runs in one of two modes. You choose in the setup card, and can change it later on Configure ▸ Mapping.

Passive: your controller's own script

The controller keeps the Live script it came with and behaves exactly as it does without MidiMirror. MidiMirror runs beside it and shows what every control is doing, with names and values read from Live.

  • Nothing to map or learn.
  • Follows the controller's mode button (Device, Volume, Pan, Sends) and its Session and Drum pad modes. A custom or user mode on the hardware greys out, because MidiMirror cannot read it.
  • The Drop works, from the footer or ⌃⌥D.
  • You cannot change what a control does. The factory script decides that.

Controllers: Novation Launchkey MK3 (37, 49, 61, 88), Launchkey Mini MK3, Launchkey MK4 (49, 61), Launchkey Mini MK4, Launch Control XL MK1 and MK2, Launch Control 3, Launch Control; Akai MIDI Mix, APC mini, APC mini mk2, APC40, APC40 mkII, LPD8; Korg nanoKONTROL2, nanoKONTROL Studio; Behringer X-Touch Compact; Arturia BeatStep. On the APC40 and APC40 mkII you can also click the mirror to stop clips and tap tempo.

Active: MidiMirror drives it

MidiMirror's own Remote Script drives the controller. You pick a mapping, or build your own, and the panel shows what every control does.

  • Choose what every knob, fader and key does, in as many modes as you want.
  • A saved knob order for each device.
  • The Drop, from the footer or a key on the controller.
  • Device Lock, Instrument mode, and JamWare mode for Chordinator and MutationStation.

Controllers: Novation Launch Control XL MK3, Launchkey MK3 37 and Akai MIDI Mix, all three tested on the hardware. Also Launch Control XL MK1 and MK2, APC mini, APC mini mk2, Korg nanoKONTROL2, nanoKONTROL Studio, Behringer X-Touch Compact and Arturia BeatStep: their layouts are drawn, but I have not had them on the bench, so confirm their controls with Learn. Any other controller starts from the Generic setup, which you teach one control at a time.

Moving a Passive controller to Active takes three steps, which the app lists for you: pick its MidiMirror row in Live's settings, restart Live, then restart MidiMirror.

The mappings it comes with (Active)

In Active mode you pick a mapping on Configure ▸ Mapping. Two come with the app. Both are read-only: change anything and MidiMirror offers you an editable copy first, so the original is always there to go back to.

JamWare: every Active controller

Listed as "MidiMix (JamWare)", "nanoKONTROL2 (JamWare)" and so on. It is one mapping that fits itself to each controller, so a controller with no faders gets a smaller mixer. Four modes:

  • Device. The knobs follow the device Live's blue hand points at, in the same order on every instrument: knob 1 filter cutoff, 2 resonance, 3 filter envelope amount, then the device's own parameters. The faders hold the amp and filter envelopes.
  • Mixer/Sends. Send A and Send B on two knob rows, each track's Utility output on the third, the faders on volume. The bank keys move the eight-track window.
  • Free. Nothing is assigned, and every control goes to Live's own MIDI map, so you can MIDI-learn anything.
  • JamWare. The panel becomes the front panel of Chordinator or MutationStation (below).

Two layer keys put one knob row on the selected track's sends, or on the Utility, Pitch, EQ and Auto Filter every track carries, without leaving the mode. The master fader stays on the selected track's volume in every mode.

Launch Control XL MK3 (Factory)

On the XL MK3 only. Novation's own layout for the controller, driven by MidiMirror so every control is named with its value.

  • Knob rows 1 and 2 on Send A and Send B, row 3 on pan.
  • The faders on volume.
  • The upper buttons solo and the lower buttons mute.
  • The NOVATION key is the Drop.

It has no other modes. Switch to the JamWare mapping for those.

Every device keeps its own layout (Active)

Save a knob layout for each device. Found the right setup for a device, like Live's Analog or a plugin like Diva, Serum or a TB-303? Maybe you want the filter cutoff always on knob 1 and the ADSR on the faders. Save it, and MidiMirror remembers that order for the device, so each parameter lands in the same place every time.

The layout belongs to the device, so the order you chose for Diva comes back on every Diva, in every project. Use Swap order to drag knobs onto each other until the row is how you want it, then press the button to save it. A Forget button on a saved device goes back to Live's own order.

A guided setup that fills in Live's settings for you

Configure opens on a three-step card. Choose your controller, choose Passive or Active, and the third step shows what Live's Control Surface settings (Settings ▸ Link, Tempo & MIDI) should say. The Your Live Setup box counts the rows you need, checks how many are already right, and spells out each one: name, input and output. Set these up for me writes them for you.

The Controller tab shows the controller in use, and from there you can open a second controller in its own window, edit the deck, or make an editable copy of a factory controller so the original stays as it is. Every controller you have opened is listed underneath, to reopen or forget.

Make your own mapping (Active)

Double-click any control and choose its job: a mixer function on this strip or a named track, a parameter of the selected device by position or by name, an envelope stage found by name on the instrument in focus, or one exact parameter on one exact device on one exact track.

Your own modes. Add, rename, hide and delete modes. A new mode starts empty, and what a control does belongs to its mode, so a knob can do one thing while you mix and another while you design sounds.

Holds and combos. A key can do one thing when tapped and another when held. Two keys pressed together can do a third. You can also give a control a key on your computer keyboard.

Or let an AI build it. Say in a sentence what you want, like "transport on the bottom row, sends on the top knobs". Press Build with AI on Configure ▸ Mapping and MidiMirror copies a full description of your controller and everything it can control. Paste that into the AI assistant you already use (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), then paste its answer back. MidiMirror checks it, refuses anything that does not exist, and saves it as a new mapping you can edit like any other. MidiMirror itself sends nothing anywhere, and there is no account or key to set up.

Teach it any controller. Press Learn on a control and move the real knob, and its address is recorded. Edit Layout moves, hides and adds controls, so the panel matches your hardware.

Generate a Remote Script for a controller you built or changed. Give it a name and MidiMirror writes a real Remote Script into Live's User Library, so it appears in Live's Control Surface list as its own entry, with everything you built in it. The controllers above already have theirs and skip this step.

The Drop

Press one key and the panel remembers where every knob and fader is. Build the track up, sweep the filters, ride the sends, push it all into the red, then press the same key again and everything snaps back at once. It works in Passive and Active.

While a drop is stored, each control shows an orange mark where it will return to.

Hold the drop key to re-aim it. Keep it held, move things to where you want them to snap back to, and let go. Nothing jumps when you do.

Two controllers, two windows

Run two controllers at once, each in its own Control Surface slot in Live with its own generated script, and you get two windows, one per controller, side by side. Each draws its own hardware, keeps its own saved setups, layout and modes, and listens only to its own MIDI.

Move either window and its place is remembered, per controller and per screen. A window opens when its controller shows up in Live and closes when it goes, without a restart.

JamWare mode: your controller plays the other apps

JamWare mode is the fourth mode of the JamWare mapping, so it needs Active. Switch to it and every knob, fader and key controls Chordinator or MutationStation instead, labelled with live values.

It follows whichever app you clicked into last. The app's groups take whole columns of the panel, and a key linked to a switch lights up when it's on, whichever side you flip it from.

The connection to the app is only open while this mode is showing.

Features

  • Names and values from Live. Every control on the panel shows the parameter it holds and its current value, in both modes.
  • Device Lock. Lock the blue hand to one device and keep selecting other tracks without your knobs following. Active.
  • Instrument mode. Device mode follows the selected track's instrument instead of the blue hand, so the knobs land on the synth every time you change track. Active.
  • A knob order for each device, saved once and used in every set. Active.
  • Build a mapping with AI. Describe the layout you want, paste MidiMirror's brief into any AI assistant and paste the reply back. It becomes a saved mapping, and anything the reply invents is refused.
  • Mappings move between controllers. Any saved mapping loads onto any controller, and lands only the controls that controller has.
  • Two controllers, two windows, each drawing its own hardware.
  • Undo, saved setups, and a "This setup" page that lists every control in every mode, what it does and the note or CC it sends, read from the script itself.

Why standalone

MidiMirror runs as its own app, not a plugin, and reaches Live only through the Remote Script it generates, the same way a hardware controller's own script does. If Live crashes, MidiMirror and your panel layout are safe. If MidiMirror hits a problem, Live keeps playing the set.

Requirements

Ableton Live is required. This is the one app in the range that does not work with other DAWs. Parameter names, the modes, the device in focus and the lock all come from a Remote Script, and only Live loads Remote Scripts.

macOS 12 or later on Apple Silicon. A supported controller, or any MIDI controller you are willing to teach with Learn. The lists are under Passive and Active above.

Intel Macs: untested, should work.

Setting it up

Open MidiMirror and click Set up my controller. Pick your controller, pick Passive or Active, and let Your Live Setup work out the Control Surface rows Live needs and offer to write them. Restart Live, and open the mirror on your second screen.

Move a knob and its name appears. Select another device and every label follows.
