Chordinator Coming soon

128 chords under one keybed, and a chord generator that can follow the root of your bassline.

Like MutationStation, Chordinator is built on "controlled randomness", here applied to chords, with a whole chord under each key. The white keys are the seven degrees of your scale and the black keys are inversions of their neighbours. Inversions can also shift on their own or live from a knob on your MIDI controller. So you can jam across 128 chords without being an expert in harmony, and find happy accidents outside the chords and voicings you usually reach for. It suits dub house and dub techno especially, where inverted chord stabs often carry both the harmony and the melody, but it fits any genre.

Controlled randomness

Chordinator has the same Elektron-style lock as MutationStation.

Click a note or a chord to lock it and the generators work around it. Lock the four chords of a progression, for example, and roll new rhythms under them as often as you like.

Auto-Lock picks the notes worth keeping for you, by downbeats, accents, long notes and a few other ways. Velocity locks are separate, so a note can keep its dynamics while its pitch is free.

Or mark an area. Draw a box around a few notes and Re-Roll changes only those. Chord Gen always rolls the whole timeline, though.

Before any generator runs, the current roll is saved in the History strip, so you can click back to a take from a few rolls ago.

128 chords under one keybed

The trigger keyboard covers the whole MIDI range in octave blocks, one chord family per block, from triads and 7ths up to sus, add, quartal and quintal chords. The white keys in a block are the seven degrees of your scale and the black keys are inversions of their neighbours.

So every key fits your scale. Play an octave up and you get the same progression with richer chords. Play the black key beside a chord for a different voicing of it. Keys you haven't mapped play single notes, and right-clicking a key takes it out of the generators' pool.

The chord generator

Each algorithm is a set of rules for which chord can follow which, rather than a stored progression. Chord Gen takes the rhythm already in the piano roll and writes chords to fit it, and two rolls of one algorithm give two different progressions in the same style.

There are 84, grouped into functional, colour, dub and minimal, house, trance, and a top-line group where the highest note carries a melody. 20 of them are measured from reference tracks.

Follow Bass only picks chords on the root your bassline is playing. Suggest uses the same algorithm without rolling, and lights up good next chords on the keybed while you play by hand.

The library, the visualiser and the chord editor are for when the keybed doesn't have what you want. Search chord types by name and drag one onto a key or into the piano roll. The visualiser names what you just played, and its Edit mode lets you play in any chord and put it on a key. The smoothing switch voices every chord as close to the tonic chord as it can.

Take the rhythm from a clip, audio clips too

↓ Rhythm Import takes the timing of the clip Live is showing and puts it in the roll as one line on the scale root, ready for Chord Gen. Point it at your drum loop and the chords follow its groove.

It works on audio clips too. Chordinator reads the sample from disk and finds the hits itself.

Warped clips are read through their warp map, so the hits land where you see them in Live. On a MIDI clip a chord counts as one hit.

Jamming on the chord keybed

  • One key per chord, across all 128 notes. If your keyboard is smaller, connect several MIDI sources and set each keyboard to a different octave, so all 128 chords are in reach without switching octaves.
  • Unmapped keys pass through. A key with no chord plays its own single note, like a normal keyboard.
  • No stuck notes. Releasing a key releases its chord, and notes shared between overlapping chords are tracked, so two held chords with a common note never leave one hanging.
  • Humanise. Loosen a chord's timing, velocity or both, so a stab doesn't land in perfect unison.
  • Velocity that follows your hand. Passed through by default, with per-voice scaling and a Fixed mode when you want every stab to land the same.
  • Suggestions for where to go next. After a chord plays, two or three keybed chords light up as good next steps, based on the chosen algorithm.
  • Transpose the whole keybed to the key your song is in.

Building the sequence

  • A poly piano roll with draw, marquee-select, halve, duplicate, invert and zoom. Each chord's name shows in the visualiser and right above it in the roll.
  • A rhythm generator with three families: dub house (offbeat and bouncy, measured from five reference tracks), dub techno (sparse, Basic Channel style) and piano house (offbeat 8ths, dotted 8ths, Charleston and tresillo figures).
  • Groove, separate from humanise. 16 groove algorithms that set timing and velocity per step.
  • Fit stabs to a bassline. Point it at a bass pattern, a Live clip or an audio file you drop on it, and it places stabs on the strongest hits to back them up, or around them so the stab answers the bass.
  • A library of saved takes. Save what's in the sequencer under a name, star favourites, sort them into folders, and step through the list with ◀ ▶ to audition them in order. It's kept in its own file with a rolling backup, apart from your settings.
  • Layouts save as JSON files, with the whole keybed, every trigger and every voicing setting.

Why standalone

Chordinator runs as its own app, not a plugin, and reaches your DAW like a hardware box: over a virtual MIDI port, the way any outboard MIDI gear does. If your DAW crashes, Chordinator and the sequence you were building are safe. If Chordinator crashes, your session keeps playing.

Requirements

macOS 12 or later on Apple Silicon, and any MIDI controller to play it from.

It works with any DAW over virtual MIDI. The clip bridge and the modulation lanes need Ableton Live.

Intel Macs: untested, should work.

Signed and notarised for macOS.

Donation-based: licence and updates

Setting it up in your DAW

1. Open Chordinator. The banner confirms the virtual output Chordinator is running.
2. Pick your controller under Trigger keyboard.
3. In your DAW, add a MIDI track, set its input to Chordinator, and arm it.

Play a trigger key and the whole chord plays in Live. Layouts save to a file, so you can keep one per song.

Extra integration with Live

Pull the clip you're looking at into the roll, and send it back as a new clip or over the old one.

Eight modulation lanes. Draw a curve, link it to any parameter in the set, and it's written into the clip as an envelope.

Presets and record. One click draws a curve across three parameters, including a set for dub stabs that ride the echo's input. Or arm a lane and record a knob move from Live.

The Remote Script (shared with MutationStation and TouchXY) installs itself on first launch. No Max for Live needed.

Control it from MidiMirror

In MidiMirror's JamWare mode, your controller becomes Chordinator's front panel. Every knob, fader and key is mapped and labelled, with nothing to set up.
