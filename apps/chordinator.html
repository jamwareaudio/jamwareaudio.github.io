Chordinator Coming soon

128 chords under one keybed, and a chord generator that can follow the root of your bassline.

Like MutationStation, Chordinator is built on "controlled randomness", here applied to chords, with a whole chord under each key. The white keys are the seven degrees of your scale and the black keys are inversions of their neighbours. Inversions can also shift on their own or live from a knob on your MIDI controller. So you can jam across 128 chords without being an expert in harmony, and find happy accidents outside the chords and voicings you usually reach for. It suits dub house and dub techno especially, where inverted chord stabs often carry both the harmony and the melody, but it fits any genre.

Controlled randomness

Generators are only useful if you can keep the parts that already work. Chordinator uses the same lock system as MutationStation, the one Elektron sequencers made famous.

Click a note or a chord to lock it and the generators work around it. Rhythm Gen replaces only unlocked notes. Chord Gen keeps the rhythm and re-rolls which chord each step gets. Fit Stabs rewrites the roll against a bassline. Lock the four chords of the progression, then roll the rhythm under them as often as you like.

Auto-Lock marks the notes worth keeping for you, scored eight ways: structural, recurring, phrase corners, downbeats, offbeats, accents, long notes or low notes. Velocity locks are separate, so a note can keep its dynamics while its pitch stays free.

Or limit it to a marked area. Draw a box around a few notes and Re-Roll changes only those hits, keeping every other one as it is. The status line says how many it kept. Chord Gen cannot do this, since it always rolls the whole timeline. Locks and the box work together: the dice never touch a locked note, in any generator.

The generators overwrite the roll on purpose, so before any of them runs, the current state is saved in the History strip. The take you wanted is often two rolls back, further than undo is comfortable, and it is still there as a picture you can click.

128 chords under one keybed

The trigger keyboard covers the whole MIDI range in octave blocks, one chord family per block: triads, 7ths, 9ths, 11ths, sus chords, add chords, 6/9 and triad-4, power 5ths, quartal and quintal. In a block the white keys are the seven degrees of your scale and the black keys are inversions of their neighbours, so moving one finger changes the voicing, not the harmony.

That is why no note on it is wrong. Play an octave up and you play the same progression in a richer flavour. Play the black key beside a chord and you get the same chord voiced differently. Keys you have not mapped play as ordinary single notes, and right-clicking a key takes it out of the pool the generators use.

The chord generator

The algorithms are not stored progressions. Each one is a table of allowed moves: for each scale degree, the two or three degrees it may go to next, best first. Chord Gen takes the rhythm already in the piano roll and writes chords to fit it, so two rolls of one algorithm give two different progressions in the same style, not the same loop twice.

There are 84, grouped by use: functional (strong steps, cadence pull, circle of 5ths), colour (suspension chains, upper structures, neo-soul 9ths, quartal drift, Dorian), dub and minimal, house, trance, and a top-line group of nine that keep the bottom notes still while the highest note carries a melody. 20 of them were measured from reference tracks rather than written from memory.

Hold sets where a chord change may land and Loop sets when the progression repeats. Follow Bass makes the algorithm pick only chords on that root: your bassline sets the root, the engine sets the harmony. Suggest uses the same algorithm with no dice at all. It lights recommended chords on the keybed, to help you explore progressions by hand.

The library, the visualiser and the chord editor are for when the keybed does not have what you want. Search every chord type by name and drag it onto the keybed or into the piano roll. The visualiser names and shows the chord or note you just played, and its Edit mode lets you play in a chord the library lacks and put it on a key. Next to them are Invert, Octave, Humanise, the voicing mode and the smoothing switch, which work on one key or a whole block. The smoothing switch voices every chord as close as possible to the tonic chord.

Take the rhythm from a clip · audio clips too

↓ Rhythm Import takes the timing of the clip Live is showing and puts it in the roll as a single line on the scale root, the same shape a Rhythm Gen pattern makes, so Chord Gen can roll chords straight onto it. Point it at your drum loop and the progression follows that groove instead of a grid.

It works on audio clips as well as MIDI. Live cannot pass audio to an app, so Chordinator reads the sample from disk and finds the hits itself, by looking for sudden jumps in the spectrum from one moment to the next.

Jamming on the chord keybed

  • One key per chord, across all 128 notes. If your keyboard is smaller, connect several MIDI sources and set each keyboard to a different octave, so all 128 chords are in reach without switching octaves.
  • Unmapped keys pass through. A key with no chord plays its own single note, so the keyboard still works as a keyboard.
  • Clean note-offs, always. Releasing a key releases its chord, and notes shared between overlapping chords are tracked, so holding two chords with a common note never leaves a note stuck on.
  • Humanise. Loosen a chord's timing, velocity or both, so a stab does not land in perfect machine unison.
  • Velocity that follows your hand. Passed through by default, with per-voice scaling and a Fixed mode when you want every stab to land the same.
  • Suggestions for where to go next. After a chord plays, two or three keybed chords light up as good next steps, based on the chosen algorithm.
  • Transpose the whole keybed to the key your song is in.

Building the sequence

  • A poly piano roll you can fully edit: draw, marquee-select, halve, duplicate, invert, zoom. Each chord's name shows in the visualiser and right above it in the roll.
  • A rhythm generator with three families: dub house (offbeat and bouncy, measured from five reference tracks), dub techno (Basic Channel style, where the space matters) and piano house (offbeat 8ths, dotted 8ths, Charleston and tresillo figures).
  • Groove, separate from humanise. 16 groove algorithms that set timing and velocity per step.
  • Fit stabs to a bassline. Point it at a bass pattern, a Live clip or an audio file you drop on it, and it places stabs on the strongest hits to back them up, or around them so the stab answers the bass.
  • A library of saved takes. Save what is in the sequencer under a name, star favourites, sort them into folders, and step through the list with ◀ ▶ to audition them in order. It is kept in its own file with a rolling backup, apart from your settings.
  • Layouts save as JSON: the whole keybed, every trigger and every voicing setting.

Why standalone

Chordinator runs as its own app, not a plugin, and reaches your DAW like a hardware box: over a virtual MIDI port, the way any outboard MIDI gear does. If your DAW crashes, Chordinator and the sequence you were building are safe. If Chordinator crashes, your session keeps playing.

Requirements

macOS 12 or later on Apple Silicon, and any MIDI controller to play it from.

Chords go out over a virtual MIDI port any DAW can listen to, and sequences import and export as standard MIDI files. The clip bridge and the modulation lanes need Ableton Live. Everything else works anywhere.

Intel Macs: beta. The app is built for Intel too and should work, but it has not been tested on an Intel Mac yet.

Signed and notarised for macOS, so it opens with a normal double-click, with no security warning.

Setting it up in your DAW

1. Open Chordinator. The banner confirms the virtual output Chordinator is running.
2. Pick your controller under Trigger keyboard.
3. In your DAW, add a MIDI track, set its input to Chordinator, and arm it.

Play a trigger key on your controller and the whole chord plays in Live. Layouts save and load as JSON, so you can keep a set of chords per song, per set or per gig.

Extra integration with Live

Pull the clip you are looking at into the roll, and send it back, as a new clip or replacing the one that is there.

Eight modulation lanes. Draw a curve as steps, slides or bends, with shape templates and its own length, link it to any parameter on any device in the set, and it is written into the clip as an envelope. Live's own knobs move, with no CC stream and nothing left running.

Presets and record. One click draws a ready-made curve across three parameters at once, including a set made for dub chord stabs that ride the echo's input so the tail keeps ringing. Save your own to the list. Or arm a lane, move a knob in Live, and the lane draws your move.

A bundled Remote Script (shared with MutationStation and TouchXY) installs itself into Live's User Library on first launch, with no Max for Live or AppleScript needed.

Control it from MidiMirror

MidiMirror can switch a controller into JamWare mode and hand it to Chordinator. Every knob, fader and key gets this app's own parameters, named and with live values, so the MidiMirror panel becomes Chordinator's front panel, with nothing to map by hand.
