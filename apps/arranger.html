Arranger

Session loops into a full arrangement, from a template.

The gap between "I have eight good loops" and "I have a song" is an hour of dragging clips. Arranger closes it: describe the song as a section list, say which kinds of track play in which section, and it lays every track's Session clip out across the Arrangement timeline in one pass.

Not released yet

Arranger exists as an Ableton Live Extension, built against the beta Extensions SDK. The rest of the range is standalone, and this one is being reworked to match before it goes on sale, so there is nothing to buy here yet.

Want it? Say so. It is the clearest signal for what gets finished next.

What it does

  • A section list you edit. Intro 8, Verse 16, Chorus 16, Break 8. Labels and bar lengths, as many sections as the song needs.
  • Roles, guessed from your track names. Every track is listed with a role already filled in (drums, bass, chords, lead, vocal, fx or other), and you correct the ones it got wrong.
  • A rule table decides who plays where. Drums come in at the verse, the lead sits out the intro, everything hits at the chorus. Change the rules, rebuild the arrangement.
  • MIDI and audio both tile properly. MIDI note data is repeated to fill each section; audio uses Live's own loop tiling. A two-bar loop fills a sixteen-bar chorus either way.
  • One pass, whole song. Right-click any track → Arrange Song from Template… and it operates on the entire set, not just the track you clicked.

Requirements

As it stands today: Ableton Live 12.4.5 Suite (beta) with the Extensions beta feature enabled. That requirement is exactly why it is being reworked, because a beta build of Live Suite is far too narrow a thing to ask for.

How it will work

It reads the first non-empty Session View clip on each track, so the workflow is: get your loops sitting in Session, then run it once and edit the arrangement it gives you.

Nothing above is speculative. The engine described here is built and working. What is unsettled is how it reaches you.
